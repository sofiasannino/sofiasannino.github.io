This study presents a two-dimensional computational fluid dynamics (CFD) analysis of a
simplified Formula One racing car geometry, with the objective of investigating the aerodynamic flow
features responsible for downforce generation, using ANSYS Fluent. The numerical simulations focus on key physical quantities such as pressure distribution, velocity field, and lift coefficient in order to characterize the global flow behavior around the vehicle. Particular attention is given to the extreme
scenario in which aerodynamic forces could, in principle, sustain the car against gravity when driving on the ceiling, providing a conceptual assessment of whether sufficient negative lift may be generated
under high-speed conditions. A systematic mesh refinement and convergence study is conducted to assess the numerical consistency
and reliability of the adopted approach. Although the inherent limitations of a two-dimensional model and the absence of three-dimensional effects are acknowledged, the study aims to provide qualitative insights into the aerodynamic mechanisms at play. The simulations are performed under steady-state conditions, using boundary conditions representative of a wind tunnel setup, and a turbulence modeling
strategy guided by near-wall resolution requirements. The final converged solution yields a negative lift coefficient of approximately CL ≈ −1.20, dominated by pressure forces and primarily generated by strong ground-effect-induced pressure gradients beneath the car. Despite the simplified two-dimensional framework, the results indicate that, under extreme high-speed conditions, the predicted aerodynamic
downforce is sufficient to overcome gravitational forces, supporting the conceptual feasibility of inverted driving in an idealized scenario.
